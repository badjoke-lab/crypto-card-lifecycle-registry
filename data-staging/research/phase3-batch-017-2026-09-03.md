## Candidate 1 — Mercuryo Spend

First-party evidence:
- https://www.mercuryo.io/spend
- https://help.mercuryo.io/hc/en-gb/articles/19135873152541-What-is-Spend
- https://help.mercuryo.io/hc/en-gb/articles/19136260065565-Where-can-I-use-my-Spend-card

Supported claims:
- Mercuryo Spend is a consumer-facing virtual payment card product.
- Mercuryo documents Spend as a virtual Mastercard that can be topped up with crypto and used for everyday card payments.
- Current support material states worldwide Mastercard acceptance where supported and no physical card.
- Current B2B/product material describes a multi-chain debit-card product and current EEA availability for card issuance.

Canonical boundary:
- Promote as a distinct active program.
- Mastercard network relationship is supported.
- Do not infer a regulated issuer from the reviewed sources unless a first-party legal disclosure identifying that entity is found before canonical write.

## Candidate 2 — Kolo Card

First-party evidence:
- https://kolo.in/cryptowallet
- https://cdn.kolo.in/public/terms-of-use
- https://cdn.kolo.in/public/privacy-policy

Supported claims:
- Kolo Card is a crypto payment card integrated with Kolo wallet services.
- Kolo documents crypto-funded spending and Apple Pay / Google Pay usage.
- Current Kolo Terms identify Card Services as provided in collaboration with Signify Holdings Inc. and link that provider to Rain Cards.
- Current terms also document a service-provider transition for crypto-asset services from Cryppo, UAB through 2025-12-31 to BURVIX SP. Z O. O. from 2026-01-01; this is not automatically a card-issuer transition.

Canonical boundary:
- Promote as a distinct active program only with roles separated.
- Reuse existing Rain provider only for the exact card-service relationship supported by current terms; do not convert the collaboration wording into an issuer role without explicit evidence.
- Preserve the 2026 crypto-service legal-entity transition separately from card infrastructure.

## Candidate 3 — Holyheld

First-party evidence:
- https://holyheld.com/
- https://holyheld.com/doc/Mover%20Final%20Report%202022.11.22.pdf

Supported claims:
- Holyheld currently presents a non-custodial consumer account with virtual and physical debit-card spending, Apple Pay / Google Pay, crypto-to-fiat top-up and personal IBAN functionality.
- Holyheld states it does not hold users' crypto or private keys.
- The 2022 Sherlock review documents the historical on-chain top-up contract path used to move funds toward debit-card settlement.

Canonical boundary:
- Promote as a distinct active program if the current product identity remains consistent at canonical write time.
- Do not assign a current issuer or network merely from generic Visa-rails marketing copy; exact provider/network relations remain unresolved until a first-party disclosure ties them to the consumer program.
- The 2022 security review is architecture evidence, not proof that the same settlement stack remains current.

## Promotion gate

For all three candidates:
- program identity must be first-party supported;
- provider and network roles must be endpoint-specific and evidence-backed;
- unresolved issuer/provider roles remain unresolved rather than inferred;
- event dates are added only where a first-party source supports an actual dated lifecycle milestone;
- validator endpoint/evidence requirements must pass before merge.
